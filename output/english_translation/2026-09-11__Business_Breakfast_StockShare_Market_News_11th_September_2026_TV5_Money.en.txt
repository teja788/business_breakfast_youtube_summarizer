[Vasanth:] Good morning. Welcome to Business Breakfast, presented by LIC, Nagarjuna's Vishishta, The Dam Good Cement, One Finger Revolution, and Capstone Home Services. It is Friday morning and we are entering the weekend. Global cues are not supportive for equities. Brent crude is near $110; after rising 7.5% last night and touching $110, it cooled slightly to about $108. NYMEX crude is at $102.73.

There are two main reasons. First, the Iran-backed Houthis have reportedly captured a key Yemeni port city on the Red Sea. They oppose Yemen's government and Iran supports them. Just as the Strait of Hormuz is critical, a great deal of goods and crude also moves along the Red Sea. The Houthis are trying to obstruct that route. Second, Saudi Arabia's East-West oil pipeline, also near a Red Sea port, was attacked, though it is not clear whether the Houthis or someone else carried it out. Most Saudi oil exports move through this route. These threats to both Hormuz and the Red Sea have driven crude to extreme levels.

Donald Trump said yesterday that the war and high oil prices would continue until the US midterm polls. There are another eight weeks, or about two months, until the November elections. Another worrying development is the global bond sell-off. The US 10-year yield is around 4.59%-4.967%--we had considered even 4.7% very high--and the 30-year yield is 5.37%, a 19-year high. The US Treasury's auction received less demand than expected. This problem has spread to the UK, Germany and other developed markets. It is unfavorable for equities. India cannot remain insulated while global markets are in turmoil. The Nifty is already in a bear market and the situation could deteriorate further.

The NSE IPO is coming. I paused briefly because I was trying to follow that news as it arrived. The offer was initially planned at Rs 30,000 crore but has been cut to Rs 22,562 crore. At the Rs 1,700-1,785 price band, NSE is valuing itself near Rs 4.42 lakh crore. In other words, NSE has indirectly told the market what it believes it is worth. Many buyers in the unlisted market paid above Rs 2,000, sometimes Rs 2,300-2,500. I met an HNI last night who bought 100,000 shares at about Rs 2,250; at Rs 1,785, the immediate notional loss runs into several crores. He said, "I will never receive all those shares through an IPO allotment, so I bought them in the unlisted space and am willing to take that risk." The listing price may still reach the unlisted level depending on the grey-market premium, so there is no need to conclude immediately that every buyer will lose.

The IPO could be subscribed 10-20 times. On a trailing-P/E basis, BSE is near 48 times while NSE is about 42.89 times at Rs 1,785, so NSE appears cheaper. The issue opens on September 17. The market is split: large caps, Nifty, Bank Nifty and Sensex on one side, and active midcaps, small caps and IPOs on the other. Most recent listings produced gains. Among the latest 10-15 listings, only three or four--Phicomm Technologies, Palak Technologies, Race of Belief and Fly High Maritime--were in losses. Farm Pees, Deepa Jewellers, Shanti Inorganics, Ashutosh Fibres, Priority Jewels, Complete Sports & Management and especially ESDS did well; Purple Style remained near issue price.

Investor behavior has changed. It has become a case of "the old is stale and the new is novel." Old favorites such as Reliance, HDFC, ITC, PI Industries, Suzlon, BSE and LIC Housing have been left behind. Lifestyles have changed through digital payments and food delivery from Zomato and Swiggy; similarly, the market now prefers new businesses. At a child's birthday dinner last night, only chicken biryani and curd rice were traditional items--they did not even serve vegetable biryani--and the rest were pasta, pizza and burgers. Just as food habits and daily life have changed, market behavior has changed. If we refuse to notice it, we will be left behind. Retail investors and HNIs are not buying the old names. Investors now accept higher valuations for Lenskart, Urban Company, ESDS Software, Milky Mist, Indo-MIM, GNG Electronics and Studymac Mechatronics instead of buying an old company simply because it trades at 10, 15 or 20 P/E.

This is a behavioral change driven by new investors and many new demat accounts. Equity mutual-fund buying has risen 19% and SIPs reached a new high. Mutual funds and AMCs must follow retail and HNI preferences if they want inflows into existing schemes and NFOs. Including NSE and a possible Rs 35,000 crore Jio Platforms issue, IPOs may collect around Rs 1 lakh crore this year.

Money is moving toward IPOs, new listings and themes such as defence, aerospace, engineering, data centers, pharma and health care. These are the dominant themes now. Pharma and health care are in their own bull market. Power and energy retain interest because they support data centers. Infrastructure, banks, financials and core sectors such as cement are behind. Even renewables have slipped after being called an emerging theme, and railways and the Vande Bharat theme have also fallen back. Themes and preferences keep changing. Investors are making money in IPOs and buying unlisted shares. There is nothing wrong with that. Conditions change with time, and we too must change; there is no alternative. Mutual-fund inflows into midcaps and small caps are at a four-month high, and the number of demat accounts has risen. We must see how investors receive NSE and where it lists.

NSE will list on BSE. BSE itself looks progressively weaker. Yesterday's cash-market distortion was again visible: during the Sensex weekly-options expiry the Sensex rose about 100 points, and the Nifty moved from roughly 23,389 to 23,484, nearly 90 points. The present market requires very selective, stock-specific action.

[Vasanth:] Kutumba Rao, what is your view of this situation?

[Kutumba Rao:] The IPO boom is not surprising. Remember that history repeats in the stock market. Anyone who has spent more than three decades in Indian markets has already seen several IPO booms. In 1994-95 and the peak year 1995-96 there was a frenzy of roughly 1,300-1,400 IPOs a year. After that fizzled out came the dot-com bubble around 2000, when many small technology and media startups listed and most later disappeared. In 2008 there were mega PSU issues such as Coal India and Reliance Power. Coal India survived; everyone knows what happened to Reliance Power and the losses it left investors.

Could the Jio Platforms issue mark the end of this IPO boom? Reliance issues have historically arrived near the end of IPO cycles, so it is possible. In 2021-22, digital-adoption stories brought roughly Rs 1.5 lakh crore of issues, including Zomato, Nykaa, Paytm and later LIC, before the market fizzled again. From 2024 until now there have already been close to 1,000 IPOs. The difference this time is strong SME participation and mostly private-sector issues, apart from the NSE issue.

Do not get carried away. Examine valuations. If, after one or two quarters following listing, performance justifies the valuation, then buy. Apply nominally to good issues and hold if allotted, but remember that IPO booms are seasonal, arriving every five to seven years.

We have kept warning about the market; no one can say we did not. Once 24,000 broke, we said 500-600 points could be lost quickly, and that happened. The Nifty band that was 24,000-24,500 is now about 23,000-23,500, a full 1,000 points lower. Investors must understand that shift. Crude, the continuing West Asian crisis, whether the US Fed raises rates, and the rise in bond yields all matter. The confusion has increased rather than decreased. As midterms approach, the US president is quarreling with neighbors, changing tariff positions and announcing election freebies--what we in India call revdis. The proposed $5,000 "Trump dividend" for every US adult could cost $1-1.2 trillion in an economy already carrying nearly $40 trillion of debt. It is fine to follow India in useful things, but not only in election freebies. At least our central bank is managing better than the US central bank.

It may take until the New Year for this confusion to clear. We have Dussehra and Diwali holidays ahead; we must see whether markets bottom before then. Mutual-fund inflows remain much higher than expected. That support has also made investors complacent. Mid- and small-cap fund inflows reached a four-month high. Returns look good now, but will SIP inflows continue once markets flatten?

The largest immediate risk is oil at $107. That is not a small matter. India is a major importer, so the impact on the external deficit and other balances will be huge. We must see how the government controls it. If crude stays here another two or three days, petrol and diesel prices may have to rise, lifting domestic inflation as soon as domestic fuel prices are increased. Banking liquidity is already so high that the SBI chief said deployment of the FCNR money could take three or four months. That means paying interest for three or four months without deployment. Did the central bank not anticipate how much surplus liquidity the FCNR deposits would create? Will credit demand and credit outflow be sufficient? There are many such ifs and buts.

Watch the BRICS event beginning in Delhi. Collectively BRICS controls a large part of the global economy outside the US. Any major outcomes from the prime minister's discussions with China or Russia will matter. I expect a range-bound market. Excessive bearishness at this level is not warranted because VIX remains below 12 and 300-400 point Nifty swings look doubtful. PCR is in the buying zone around 0.77. Put open interest is concentrated at 23,000 and calls at 23,500, with especially heavy call writing at 23,400. The Nifty needs to sustain above 23,400.

Selective speculative buying appeared in stocks such as Cochin Shipyard, Astra, KEI and Sigachi, while Radico Khaitan, Blue Star and Ather Energy became dull. High-delivery conviction appeared in CMS, DMart, Power Grid and Hero MotoCorp. The F&O ban list contains the same five or six names--Bandhan Bank, Inox, Kaynes, Manappuram and SAIL--while LIC Housing has come out. The rupee weakened as expected to 95.44 and may attempt 96 in a third straight session. Even domestic institutions appear tired: their buying fell to only about Rs 1,000 crore, and FPIs bought only Rs 438 crore. HDFC Bank, Neogen Chemicals, Premier Energies and Lotus Developers may see action today.
