[Vasanth:] Alu Fluoride, a small Hyderabad specialty-chemicals company in a favorable cycle, announced excellent Q2 numbers. After the break we will discuss it.

[Vasanth:] Welcome back. Alu Fluoride's net profit rose 91% year on year to Rs 15.2 crore, while revenue rose 17%. Operational recovery and better margins followed the removal of Q1 raw-material problems. It is India's leading producer of aluminum fluoride. The company was promoted by Venkat Akkineni, elder son of the late actor Akkineni Nageswara Rao, and has been listed for three decades, though it is not widely known among investors.

The company declared its first interim/special dividend of Rs 2.50 per share. Aluminum is in a favorable global cycle, raw-material problems have eased, expansion plans are complete, and the company is considering a second plant abroad. Second-generation leadership is also emerging through Akkineni Aditya. Chairman Meka Yugandhar and Managing Director Akkineni Venkat joined us. The stock can be considered for portfolios, but liquidity is constrained; trying to buy 10,000 shares at once may disappoint. The five-year road map, cash flows, balance sheet and cash reserves are good, with low debt.

[Vasanth, introducing the interview:] Q2 revenue was Rs 65.2 crore, versus Rs 28.9 crore in Q1 and Rs 55 crore a year earlier. Net profit rose from Rs 2.8 crore in Q1 and Rs 8.22 crore a year earlier to Rs 15.2 crore. Quarterly EPS was Rs 19.38, against Rs 32 for the whole previous year. The company declared an interim dividend of Rs 2.50 per share; last year's only/final dividend was Rs 4. These were record revenue, EBITDA and profit figures.

[Akkineni Venkat:] In Q1, the Iran-Iraq/Middle East conflict and closure of the Strait of Hormuz disrupted the phosphoric-acid suppliers from whom we receive a waste raw material that we turn into aluminum fluoride. Our tagline is "wealth from waste." With no raw material, production suffered. Supply resumed in Q2, production increased, technical staff worked efficiently and consumption norms improved.

We had expected raw-material prices to rise, so as a cash-rich company we bought and stored material at lower prices. Prices have since risen. Our annual contracts gave us an advantage because we fixed prices when supply was ample. We also bring material from Paradip, 650 kilometers away, but an agreement with Coromandel now gives us raw material through a next-door pipeline, while Kakinada is only about 170 kilometers from Visakhapatnam. Lower raw-material and freight costs, better selling prices and efficiency all raised revenue and profit.

The global conflict raised phosphorous and other raw-material prices, and AlF3 prices rose too. The disruption affected our fertilizer-company suppliers' fluosilicic acid output. Coromandel is next door with zero transport cost and supplied nearly double the quantity in these six months, more than in the last ten years as heard. We also have long-term contracts with IFFCO at Paradip, one of the world's largest phosphoric-acid producers. India's seven smelters belong to Hindalco, Vedanta and NALCO. We listed in 1994 and first sold in 1995.

[Vasanth:] You have kept a low profile for a 32-year listed company. With aluminum customers Hindalco, NALCO and Vedanta expanding, a good opportunity is visible. This is the first interim dividend and indicates healthy cash flow. What are the next expansion plans and backward-integration projects such as alumina hydrate?

[Akkineni Venkat / management:] Expansion opportunities are always examined, but the backward-integration project has not crystallized. The company has about Rs 60 crore cash. Debt is low, not zero; finance cost was about Rs 2.89 crore last quarter as heard and may have risen. Debottlenecking can raise production and turnover. A 2.1 MW item was also mentioned as heard. The Visakhapatnam area has urbanized greatly since the plant was built.

Receivables are less problematic because annual contracts keep customers with us. Demand in India is roughly 80,000 tonnes and our capacity is 24,000 tonnes. Fluorspar demand helped pricing. Management expects the performance to continue in Q3 and Q4 because NALCO, Hindalco and Vedanta are expanding. The customers are expected to remain with Alu Fluoride even if competitors enter.

[Vasanth:] How did you enter chemicals from entertainment?

[Akkineni Venkat:] My father never forced either Nagarjuna or me into acting, production or direction. I studied MBA Finance and wanted to enter business. In 1977, at age 23, I had to manage Annapurna Studios when it was making losses. I already had Annapurna Apex Chemicals, a roughly Rs 75 lakh unit that made the white powder used in fluorescent tubes. Film production had stopped, but the studio needed production to survive. Nagarjuna chose acting, so I produced his first film. I am now 72, with nearly a 50-year career.

After 20-25 years I felt film was chaotic and that at a certain age one should hand opportunity to the next generation. I was a founding director of Alu Fluoride. About 18 years ago my mother-in-law asked me to take responsibility because no one else could. I entered reluctantly, initially thinking I would sell it after three years, but it continued and became successful.

[Vasanth:] Extrapolating the half year, the company may exceed Rs 250 crore revenue this year and perhaps reach Rs 50 EPS. It has posted about Rs 23 first-half consolidated EPS. Fluorine companies often receive 50-90 PE valuations, but Alu Fluoride is a single-product company. Can it diversify?

[Akkineni Venkat / management:] That is exactly our thought process. Multiple products are under consideration, though we cannot disclose them until finalized and approved by the board. Investors seek growth opportunities. Last-quarter capacity utilization was about 70%, yet revenue was Rs 65 crore. Higher utilization can bring operating leverage.

My son Aditya is a mechanical engineer. He worked abroad for three to three-and-a-half years, has seven or eight years of experience here as general manager, and is now the company's CEO. Succession and shared responsibility are good for any organization, and employees must be looked after. We also want to expand outside India because we have examined the viable domestic opportunities. We had supplied Dubai Aluminium but paused because Indian demand was stronger; we plan to restart. Over the next five years, Alu Fluoride can certainly aspire to become a Rs 1,000 crore company.
