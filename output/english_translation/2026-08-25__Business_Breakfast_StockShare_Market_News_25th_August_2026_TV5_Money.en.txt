[Vasanth:] This is an important Tuesday: Nifty's first monthly expiry under the new cash-settlement and closing-session system. Crude remains around $92-93. The US sanctions on Iran are largely old measures in a new package, aimed indirectly at China and Russia. Crude did not react because the story was already known. US-Canada trade tensions are another concern.

US yields remain unfriendly to equities: the 10-year near 4.7% and the 30-year near 5.2%. The dollar index is around 99. Gold gained about 15% in August and reached a three-month high, with a possible journey toward $5,000. The US plans to use about $1 trillion from its general account in a bond-buying programme; suppressing yields artificially may only defer the problem.

Nifty formed an outside day and could take a downside direction after expiry. Investors should focus on stock-specific opportunities. August brought roughly $10 billion—about Rs 95,000-96,000 crore—of IPOs, offers for sale and other paper supply, yet liquidity absorbed it. Hindustan Copper's 6% OFS began with a floor around Rs 514 and size near Rs 3,000 crore.

[Kutumba Rao:] This first combined weekly/monthly expiry under cash settlement is dangerous, especially for stock options requiring physical settlement. Retail traders should avoid major risk and observe how the precedent develops. The broader market has spent much of the last two years consolidating, with occasional large moves followed by fresh consolidation, while individual stocks show a very different picture.

Some IPOs are priced at attractive valuations and long-term investors can retain them, but avoid issues designed only to exploit investor greed. Delivery accumulation and volume can precede a stock's takeoff, but a speculative story remains risky until supported by fundamentals.

[Ramakrishna:] Traders should square off expiry positions before the last fifteen minutes and avoid the closing session. Stock options require particular caution because of physical settlement.

[Kranthi:] Lalithaa Jewellery's fundamentals appear good, but it had only just listed. Rather than deploy Rs 10 lakh at once, wait for a quarterly result and add slowly in diversified tranches. Motilal Oswal Nasdaq 100 ETF will decline if Nasdaq corrects; holders in profit should book. Vikram Solar is very weak, fell from about Rs 400 through Rs 225 to around Rs 170, and weak results reinforced the sell-on-rallies view. Interarch Building Products is a decent long-term pre-engineered-building business, but a further decline of Rs 200-300 would create a better entry.

The market opened negative with weak breadth. Hindustan Copper fell about 5% on the OFS; IIFL Finance, Welspun Corp, Alkyl Amines, Balrampur Chini, Hexaware, Acme Solar, JBM Auto and Data Patterns were among weak counters.
